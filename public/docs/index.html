Get started

Overview

JConnect makes any of your computers feel like the one in front of you. Install it, pair once, and press Connect. There are no IP addresses, ports or VPN settings to learn, and you don’t need an account.

  • Computers run the JConnect app. It shares that computer with your paired devices and connects you to your other computers.
  • Phones, tablets and TVs can use JConnect in their web browser, served by your JConnect computer. Android also has its own app.
  • Every connection is end-to-end encrypted, whichever network carries it: your local network, JVPN, Tailscale or another VPN.
Get started

Install JConnect

Windows

  1. Download the installer for Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.
  2. Open JConnect-Setup.exe. It installs for your account without admin rights, adds Start menu and desktop shortcuts, and starts JConnect.
  3. The first time JConnect starts, Windows Firewall asks whether to allow it. Allow it on private networks so your other devices can reach this computer.

The beta isn’t code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may say “Windows protected your PC”. Choose More info → Run anyway.

Portable version. JConnect-<version>-Portable.exe runs without installing. It tells you about new versions, and you download them from the website.

Android

  1. Download the Android app on a phone or tablet running Android 8.0 or later.
  2. Open JConnect-Android.apk. The first time, Android asks you to allow installing apps from your browser or file manager.
  3. Open JConnect and tap Add Computer. Scan the code your computer shows under Settings → Use this computer from a phone, or type the computer’s address and the pairing code shown at the bottom of its JConnect window.

The Android app connects to your computers. It doesn’t share the phone’s own screen.

Android TV and Google TV

The same APK installs on TVs and appears on the TV’s home screen. Install it with an app such as Downloader, or with adb install. On a TV, JConnect has a layout you move around with the remote:

  • Add Computer lists the computers on the network, so there’s no address to type.
  • In a session, the arrows move the pointer and OK clicks. Hold OK to right-click, or hold it while moving to drag.
  • Back shows the session controls. There you can switch the arrows to scrolling or to arrow keys, or open the on-screen keyboard.

Linux

Download the .deb from the download page and install it with sudo apt install ./JConnect-Linux.deb. It works on Ubuntu, Debian, Linux Mint and other Debian-based systems on 64-bit PCs, and JConnect starts when you sign in. Paired devices can control a Linux computer in an X11 session, such as Ubuntu on Xorg. Wayland sessions aren't supported yet.

Other Linux distributions

On other distributions, make the AppImage executable and open it: chmod +x JConnect-<version>-x86_64.AppImage. JConnect runs on 64-bit Intel and AMD PCs.

Linux sessions: X11 and Wayland

Paired devices can see and control a Linux computer in an X11 session, such as Linux Mint, Xfce, or Ubuntu on Xorg on Ubuntu 24.04. Wayland sessions aren’t supported yet: paired devices can’t control the computer, and screen sharing hasn’t been tested. JConnect starts when you sign in, through ~/.config/autostart, unless you turn that off in Settings.

Mac

  1. Download the DMG for your Mac from the download page: Apple silicon or Intel. Apple menu → About This Mac shows Chip on Apple silicon Macs and Processor on Intel Macs. JConnect needs macOS 13 Ventura or later.
  2. Open the DMG and drag JConnect into Applications.
  3. Open JConnect. It isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so the first time macOS says it can’t check it. Close that message, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway next to JConnect.
  4. So your paired devices can see and control the Mac, open Settings → Mac permissions in JConnect and turn on Screen Recording and Accessibility.

Mac permissions

After you open JConnect on a Mac, allow what it asks for:

  • Local Network, so your other devices can find and reach this Mac.
  • Screen Recording and Accessibility, so paired devices can see and control this Mac. JConnect lists both under Settings → Mac permissions. You don’t need them to use other computers from this Mac.

On macOS 14 and earlier you can also Control-click JConnect in Applications and choose Open instead of using Open Anyway.

iPhone and iPad

iPhone and iPad use JConnect in Safari today: open Settings → Use this computer from a phone on your computer and scan the code with the Camera app.

A JConnect app for iPhone and iPad (iOS 15 or later) is coming through TestFlight and the App Store. It works like the Android app: tap Add Computer, then scan the code or type the computer’s address. It connects to your computers and doesn’t share the iPhone’s own screen.

Raspberry Pi

The Raspberry Pi app is coming soon. Until then you can reach your computers from a Raspberry Pi through its browser, the same way as from a phone.

Closing the window

Closing the JConnect window keeps the computer available. JConnect stays in the system tray, and Start with this computer in Settings starts it in the background when you sign in.

Get started

Pair a computer

You pair each device with a computer once. After that, the computer recognizes the device by its key and you just press Connect.

  1. Install JConnect on both computers.
  2. On the computer you’re using, open Add Computer. JConnect computers on your network show up there by themselves.
  3. Choose the computer, then enter the pairing code shown at the bottom of the JConnect window on that computer and press Pair.
  4. Press Connect. You won’t need to pair again.

Ask for permission instead

If you can’t see the code, press Ask for permission. Someone at the other computer gets a prompt. Both screens show the same short verification code: they check it matches, then press Allow. Ticking This is one of my own devices marks the device as yours.

The pairing code never leaves your device. JConnect proves it knows the code over the encrypted connection, so an eavesdropper can’t learn or reuse it.

Computers that don’t show up

Under Add Computer → Other ways to connect, type the computer’s name or address and press Find. You can also import computers from Tailscale and other networks.

Get started

Connect and work

Press Connect on a computer in My Computers. The remote computer fills the window, with sound from Windows computers.

Controls

Move the pointer to the top edge, or press Ctrl Alt Home (Control Option Home on a Mac), to show the controls:

  • ← Home goes back to your computer list.
  • Display switches between the remote computer’s screens.
  • Devices switches to another of your computers.
  • ⋯ sets the quality, full screen and zoom, or disconnects.

Quality

SettingWhat you get
AutomaticSharp on your local network, Balanced everywhere else.
SharpFull resolution at 30 frames per second, up to 10 Mbps.
BalancedUp to 1920 pixels wide at 30 frames per second, up to 5 Mbps.
Data saverUp to 1280 pixels wide at 15 frames per second, up to 1.5 Mbps.

The computer you connect to also lowers the quality by itself when it’s working hard or running hot, and raises it again when it cools down.

Several devices at once

More than one paired device can use a computer at the same time, for example to work on something together or to help someone. Everyone sees the same screen, and everyone who can control it shares one pointer and keyboard.

  • The bar shows 2 people (or more). Click it to see who’s connected, whether they can control the computer, and whether they’re recording.
  • JConnect tells you when someone joins, leaves, or starts or stops recording. Phones, tablets and TVs are told too.
  • On the computer itself, Settings → Connected now lists everyone, and Disconnect ends one person’s session.

Recording a session

In a session, open ⋯ and choose Record session. The time since you started shows at the top, with a small REC badge while the controls are hidden. Click the time, or choose ⋯ → Stop recording, to stop.

  • Recordings are video with sound, saved as WebM files in Videos › JConnect (Movies › JConnect on a Mac) and named after the computer and the time. ⋯ → Show recordings and Settings → Recordings open the folder.
  • Windows, Linux and web browsers play WebM files. On a Mac, QuickTime Player doesn’t, so open them in Chrome or VLC.
  • Recording is never hidden. The computer you record shows a notification and marks you as recording under Settings → Connected now, and everyone else connected is told.
  • If the connection drops, what you recorded so far is kept, and recording carries on in a new file when JConnect reconnects. Closing the window keeps the recording too.

Sessions can be recorded only in the desktop app on Windows, macOS and Linux, not on phones, tablets or TVs. Recording has been tested on Windows.

Displays and appearance

On a computer with more than one display, Display switches between them. JConnect follows your system’s light or dark mode. To choose one yourself, open Settings → Appearance.

Stopping everything

Closing the window keeps the computer available, and JConnect stays in the system tray (the menu bar on macOS). To stop everything, press ⏻ at the top of the window, or use the tray menu:

  • Terminate JConnect ends every connection and task, stops all of JConnect’s background services and closes it.
  • Shut Down Computer turns the computer off.

Both ask first.

If the connection drops

JConnect reconnects by itself, trying every path it knows. If a paired computer is asleep and supports Wake-on-LAN, pressing Connect wakes it. You can also right-click it and choose Wake.

Organize your computers

Right-click a computer for more options:

  • Rename… changes its name, and Whose computer is it? groups computers under a person’s name.
  • Connect using… picks the network JConnect uses for it.
  • Create Shortcut puts a shortcut on your desktop that connects with a double-click.
Get started

Phones, tablets and TVs

Android, iPhone, iPad and TVs with a modern browser can use JConnect without installing anything. On Android you can also install the app, which scans the code for you. An app for iPhone and iPad is coming soon.

  1. On the computer you want to use, make sure Remote access and Allow phones and browsers are on in Settings.
  2. Open Settings → Use this computer from a phone. JConnect shows a QR code and an address.
  3. Scan the code with the phone’s camera, or type the address into a tablet or TV browser. Then tap Pair.

The phone needs to be on the same network as the computer, or on the same private network such as Tailscale. On touch screens, tap to click and pinch to zoom, and open ⋯ → Keyboard to type.

The phone page itself loads over plain http on your local network, but the connection to the computer is still end-to-end encrypted. On networks you don’t trust, use the desktop app.

Networks

JVPN

JVPN is JConnect’s own network. It’s on by default, so you don’t need another VPN.

  • At home, devices connect directly over the local network.
  • Away from home, sign in to your JConnect account on both devices. Each computer keeps an outgoing, signed connection to the JConnect Cloud relay, and your other devices reach it through that relay.
  • The relay only passes encrypted bytes. It can’t read, change or impersonate anything. Only devices on your account can see or dial each other, and every dial uses a one-minute ticket.

JVPN carries remote desktop, SSH and Remote Desktop (RDP) to your JConnect computers. Choose what each computer shares under Settings → Share through JVPN. JVPN is built into JConnect and doesn’t install a system-wide network adapter.

Networks

SSH

  • Add SSH host, or import every named host from ~/.ssh/config.
  • Open SSH terminal on a JConnect computer reaches its SSH server through JVPN. Turn on Settings → Share through JVPN → SSH on that computer, and set its SSH port if it isn’t 22.
  • Sign-in tries your SSH agent, then this device’s own JConnect SSH key, then asks for a password. Copy my SSH key copies the public key so you can add it to a server.
  • A host’s key is remembered the first time you connect. If it changes later, JConnect stops and tells you.

SSH to a JConnect computer needs an SSH server running on it, for example OpenSSH Server on Windows.

Networks

Remote Desktop

  • Add Computer → Import Remote Desktop file adds an existing .rdp connection.
  • Open with Remote Desktop opens a computer in Windows Remote Desktop. On a Mac, JConnect opens the .rdp file with your Remote Desktop app. On Linux it uses FreeRDP or Remmina.
  • To reach a Windows computer’s Remote Desktop from outside your network, turn on Settings → Share through JVPN → Remote Desktop on that computer.
Security

How it’s secured

Every connection uses JConnect protocol v2, whichever network carries it.

  • Encrypted end to end. Each connection runs a fresh X25519 key exchange. Messages, remote-control input, SSH and Remote Desktop data are sealed with XSalsa20-Poly1305. Frames are numbered, so altered, replayed or reordered data closes the connection. Screen and sound travel over WebRTC, which is DTLS-SRTP encrypted, and its keys are signed inside the channel.
  • Both sides prove who they are. Every device has an Ed25519 identity key, and its ID comes from that key. The computer signs each connection’s transcript, and the other device checks it against the key it paired with. The connecting device signs the same transcript, so a stolen or replayed message can’t be reused.
  • Secrets never cross the network. Pairing codes and passwords become scrypt proofs bound to one connection.
  • Stored safely. Device keys, API keys, account tokens and SSH keys are encrypted with the operating system’s key store.
  • Least exposure. Discovery announces only a name and public key, and you can turn it off. The app’s windows only get the permissions they need, and the Windows app is hardened with Electron fuses.
Security

Protocol v2 in depth

Every connection between JConnect devices uses the same protocol, whether it travels over your local network, JVPN, Tailscale or another VPN. These are the parts, in the order a connection uses them.

1. Fresh keys for every connection

The client and the computer each send a hello with an ephemeral X25519 key and a nonce. The keys are used once and thrown away, so recording a connection today doesn’t help anyone decrypt it later. Separate keys are derived for each direction.

2. Both sides prove who they are

Every device has an Ed25519 identity key, and its ID is derived from that key. The computer signs the transcript of this exact conversation, and the other device checks the signature against the key it paired with. The connecting device signs the same transcript, so a stolen or replayed message can’t be reused on another connection. A changed computer is caught before anything is trusted.

3. Every message is sealed and numbered

After the hello, messages, remote-control input, SSH and Remote Desktop data are sealed with XSalsa20-Poly1305. Each frame carries a counter, so altered, replayed or reordered data closes the connection. Screen and sound travel over WebRTC, which is DTLS-SRTP encrypted, and its keys are signed inside the channel.

4. Secrets never cross the network

Pairing codes and passwords are turned into scrypt proofs bound to that one connection. The code isn’t sent, and an eavesdropper can’t replay the proof. “Ask for permission” pairing shows a matching verification code on both screens.

5. Stored safely, exposed rarely

  • Device keys, API keys, account tokens and SSH keys are encrypted with the operating system’s key store (DPAPI on Windows, the Keychain on macOS).
  • Discovery announces only a name and public key, and you can turn that off.
  • The app’s windows only get the permissions they need. The packaged app has Electron fuses set: no Node mode, no inspector, and asar integrity checked.
  • Travel Mode and Emergency Lockdown still apply to every path, JVPN included.
Security

Trusted devices

Settings lists every device that can use this computer. For each one you can:

  • Choose Full control or View only.
  • Mark it as Mine, which matters for Travel Mode.
  • Remove it, which revokes its access right away.

Connected now shows active sessions, each with a Disconnect button. Recent activity under Security logs pairing, connections and anything suspicious.

Extra protection

  • Require password adds a password to every connection. Paired devices are already protected without it.
  • Trust my account’s devices lets devices signed in to your JConnect account connect without pairing. It’s off by default.
  • Hide from nearby devices stops announcing this computer on the local network. Paired devices can still connect.
  • Allow phones and browsers serves the JConnect web page for phones, tablets and TVs on your network.
Security

Travel Mode and Emergency Lockdown

Travel Mode

Turn on Travel Mode when you leave a computer unattended. New pairing turns off, streaming is capped at Balanced (Data saver on battery), and JConnect is stricter about suspicious activity. Only my own devices lets only devices marked as yours connect.

Emergency Lockdown

JConnect rates suspicious activity within a 10-minute window:

LevelWhat happensFor example
LowThe attempt is blocked and logged.An unknown device tries to connect.
MediumPairing pauses for 15 minutes and you get a notification.3 wrong pairing codes or 5 wrong passwords from one device, or a connection that fails a security check.
HighEvery session ends and remote access turns off until you restore it.A device pretends to be one you trust, 10 wrong codes or passwords in total, or one device failing security checks 3 times.

In Travel Mode these limits are halved, and repeated attempts by unknown devices count as medium. To end a lockdown, review Recent activity and press Restore Remote Access in Settings.

Emergency shutdown

If you turn on Emergency shutdown, a high-level event in Travel Mode also shuts the computer down safely after a 60-second warning.

Account

Account and sync

An account is optional. Click the person icon at the top of JConnect to sign in or create one, and choose where the account is kept.

Where your account is kept

  • JConnect Cloud is JConnect’s own server, so JVPN reaches your computers from anywhere. Signing in can take up to a minute when nobody has used it for a while.
  • This PC: JConnect on this computer keeps the account. On your other devices, choose Another PC and enter the address shown under Settings → Account → Keep accounts on this PC. They can sign in and sync while this PC is on and they’re on the same network or Tailscale.
  • Another PC is an account kept on one of your other computers.

You can also host your own JConnect Cloud.

What stays private

  • Your password stays on the device. It’s turned into two keys: one the server checks, and one that encrypts your data before upload.
  • Computers, SSH hosts and your list of devices sync between your devices, stored on the server only as ciphertext.
  • Signing in makes your computers reachable through JVPN away from home.

Two-step sign-in

Turn on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app under Account. When it’s on, changing your password or deleting your account asks for an authenticator code as well.

Change password and delete account

Both are under Account, and both ask for your password again.

  • A new password re-encrypts your synced data and signs out your other devices, which then sign in with it.
  • A forgotten password can’t be recovered, because nothing on the server can open your data.

Trusting your own devices

Trust my account’s devices (off by default) lets your own signed-in devices connect without pairing.

Account

Host JConnect Cloud

JConnect Cloud handles accounts, sync, the JVPN relay and optional TURN. JConnect has its own hosted server, and you can run your own instead. The server is part of the JConnect source code and needs Node.js.

cd server/cloud
npm install
npm start          # listens on port 47900

Options

VariableWhat it does
TLS_CERT, TLS_KEYServe https with this certificate and key. You can also put the server behind a reverse proxy.
TURN_PUBLIC_HOSTTurns on TURN to relay remote-desktop media when two networks block direct connections.
TURN_PORTThe TURN port. The default is 3478.
JCONNECT_CLOUD_DBWhere the SQLite accounts database is stored. The default is server/cloud/data/cloud.db.
DATABASE_URLA Postgres connection string. When it’s set, the data is kept there instead of in SQLite.
DATABASE_CA_CERTA PEM file’s text or path, to check the database’s certificate against your own authority.
 
TLS_CERT=/etc/jconnect/cert.pem TLS_KEY=/etc/jconnect/key.pem TURN_PUBLIC_HOST=cloud.example.com npm start

Storage

  • By default, accounts, devices and sessions are kept in an SQLite database, which needs Node.js 22.13 or later. Accounts from an older cloud.json are imported the first time the server starts, and the file is kept as cloud.json.imported.
  • With DATABASE_URL set, JConnect Cloud creates and updates its tables in a jconnect schema when it starts.
  • A Postgres database on another machine is reached over TLS. On a private network, such as Render’s internal addresses, the certificate isn’t checked, because those certificates are self-signed. Everywhere else it is. To turn TLS off on a private network, add ?sslmode=disable to the connection string.

Plain http

JConnect only accepts http:// cloud addresses on private networks. Everywhere else, use https.

Hosting on Render

  • Build command: npm ci --omit=dev --ignore-scripts && npm ci --omit=dev --prefix server/cloud
  • Start command: node server/cloud/cloud.js
  • Database: set DATABASE_URL to the Postgres database’s internal URL. JConnect Cloud won’t start on Render without it, because Render wipes a service’s disk on every deploy.
  • Free plan limits: the service sleeps after 15 minutes without requests, and a free database expires 30 days after it’s created unless it’s upgraded.

Troubleshooting

A computer doesn’t appear under Nearby

  • Check both computers are on the same network, and that JConnect is running with Remote access on.
  • Allow JConnect through the firewall on private networks. See Network ports.
  • Check Hide from nearby devices is off on the other computer.
  • Use Add Computer → Other ways to connect with its name or address, or import it from Tailscale.

“Pairing is paused for a little while”

Too many wrong codes or declined requests pause pairing for 15 minutes. Pairing is also off in Travel Mode and during Emergency Lockdown.

I can’t reach a computer away from home

Sign in to the same JConnect account on both devices, keep Use JVPN on, and check the computer is awake and online. Or use a VPN you already have, like Tailscale.

The phone page won’t open

Turn on Allow phones and browsers, put the phone on the same network or Tailscale network, and type the address exactly as JConnect shows it, including http:// and the port.

I can see the screen but can’t control it

Check your device has Full control on that computer. On Windows, apps running as administrator and UAC prompts ignore input from JConnect unless JConnect also runs as administrator.

Reference

Known limits

  • The Windows, Mac, Linux and Android apps are early betas.
  • The Windows, Mac and Linux builds aren’t code-signed or notarized, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS ask you to confirm them the first time.
  • Windows can’t be controlled on the secure desktop: UAC prompts, Ctrl+Alt+Del and the lock screen. Apps running as administrator ignore input from JConnect unless JConnect also runs as administrator.
  • Linux computers can be controlled only in X11 sessions. On Wayland, paired devices can’t control them yet, and screen sharing hasn’t been tested there.
  • A Mac can’t be controlled at its lock screen or login window, and its sound isn’t shared yet. ⌘Tab, ⌘Space and other system shortcuts stay on the Mac you’re using.
  • SSH to a JConnect computer needs an SSH server running on it, for example OpenSSH Server on Windows.
  • The phone web page is served over plain http on your local network. The connection itself is still end-to-end encrypted, but use the desktop app on networks you don’t trust.
  • On a real iPhone or iPad, the iOS app can’t list the computers on the network until Apple grants the app the multicast networking entitlement. Scanning the code or typing the address works without it.
  • A device that joins a session doesn’t see anyone else’s pointer, only the pointer on the remote screen that everyone shares.
  • Sessions can be recorded only in the desktop app on Windows, macOS and Linux, not on phones, tablets or TVs.
  • The Raspberry Pi and Apple TV apps are coming soon. iPhone and iPad work in Safari today.
  • Camera sharing isn’t built yet.
Developers

Build from source

JConnect is an Electron app with a Node.js server. These commands run from the repository root.

npm install
npm start                    # run JConnect
npm run start:b              # a second copy with its own identity, for testing on one PC
npm run dist:win             # build the Windows installer and the portable exe
npm run dist:mac             # on a Mac: build the arm64 and x64 DMGs
npm run dist:linux           # on Linux: build the AppImage and the .deb

Mobile apps

cd mobile && npm install && npm run sync && cd android && ./gradlew assembleDebug    # Android APK (needs JDK 17 to 21)
cd mobile && npm install && npm run sync:ios && open ios/App/App.xcodeproj           # on a Mac: the iOS app in Xcode 26 or later

Tests

node --test test/*.test.js src/web/test/connection.test.js server/relay/test/relay.test.js server/cloud/test/cloud.test.js
cd server/cloud && npm install && npm test   # JConnect Cloud with SQLite and with Postgres (PGlite)

Platform notes

  • Releases and updates. Build a release with the version it’s published as, so the updater can tell versions apart, for example npm run dist:win -- -c.extraMetadata.version=0.1.0-beta.2.
  • Mac. DMGs can only be built on a Mac, because they need Apple’s tools. The Mac app icon is an Icon Composer document, build/JConnect.icon, so macOS 26 and later show it in Liquid Glass. Compiling it needs Xcode 26 or later running on macOS 26 or later. Anywhere else, npm run dist:mac uses the flat assets/icon-mac.png instead.
  • iOS. The iOS app can only be built on a Mac too.
  • Linux. The packages include an input helper written in C, which needs a compiler and the X11 and XTest headers: sudo apt install build-essential libx11-dev libxtst-dev.

Glossary

My Computers
The list of computers you’ve paired with or imported. Press Connect on one to use it.
Pairing code
The 6-digit code shown at the bottom of JConnect on a computer. It’s proven over the encrypted connection and never sent.
Verification code
The short code that “Ask for permission” shows on both screens. Someone at the computer checks that they match and presses Allow.
JVPN
JConnect’s built-in network. Devices connect directly at home and through the relay away from home.
Relay
The JConnect Cloud service that passes encrypted bytes between your devices when they aren’t on the same network. It can’t read, change or impersonate them.
Dial ticket
A one-minute pass the relay issues each time one of your devices connects to another.
Protocol v2
The secure channel every JConnect connection uses, whichever network carries it.
Travel Mode
A setting for unattended computers: no new pairing, capped streaming and stricter handling of suspicious activity.
Emergency Lockdown
What JConnect does when it rates suspicious activity high: every session ends and remote access turns off until you restore it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an account?

No. An account is optional. You need one for sync and for JVPN away from home.

Do I need to open ports on my router?

No. Nothing needs to be opened to the internet. Away from home, JVPN uses outgoing connections. On a local network, allow the JConnect ports through the firewall.

Can the relay see what I do?

No. The relay only passes encrypted bytes. It can’t read, change or impersonate anything.

Does JVPN change my network settings?

No. JVPN is built into JConnect and doesn’t install a system-wide network adapter, so other apps don’t see it as a VPN.

Can two people use one computer at once?

Yes. They see the same screen and share one pointer and keyboard. Several devices at once explains how to see who’s connected.

Can I record a session?

Yes, in the desktop app. Recording is never hidden: the computer you record shows a notification, and everyone connected is told.

Does the Android or iPhone app share my phone’s screen?

No. The apps connect to your computers. They don’t share the phone’s own screen.

Why does my computer warn me about JConnect?

The builds aren’t code-signed or notarized yet, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS ask you to confirm them the first time. The install steps show where to confirm.

What if I forget my account password?

It can’t be recovered, because nothing on the server can open your data. Your devices still pair and connect without an account.
